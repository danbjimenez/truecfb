About TrueCFB

Blind to the jersey. Every game counts. Open formula.

Why

Most college football rankings start with an opinion. A preseason poll ranks teams before anyone has played. A committee meets in a room and weighs what it saw.

TrueCFB starts from the scoreboard. It knows who played, where they played, who won and by how much. It does not know a team's name, logo, conference or reputation.

Every team is scored by the same seven rules. No game is dropped and no opponent gets a special case. Anyone can run the rules and get the same ranking.

It measures the season that was played. It does not predict the next game, and a gap to a poll is a measurement, not a verdict.

How it works

Every team gets a score from 0 to 100 for each game. Its TrueCFB Team Rating is the average of those scores.

  1. Every game has a Game Rating: 100 plus both teams' bonuses. Two average teams make a 100. The two best teams make about 155. That is the pot the two teams split.
  2. The winner's share starts at 75 and the loser's at 25.
  3. Margin moves it up to 5 points either way: a 1-point win is 70, a blowout win is 80. An overtime game counts as a 1-point margin.
  4. Add 3 on the road, subtract 3 at home, nothing at a neutral site.
  5. Add your opponent's bonus, win or lose. The bonus is listed beside every team: it is how far the team is rated above or below 50. An FCS opponent counts as a 25, so its bonus is −25.
  6. Keep the result between 0 and 100. A team's rating is the average of its game scores.
  7. Postseason games are worth more, and their scores can go above 100. A regular bowl adds 10 for both teams. A playoff game adds 15 for both teams, and the winner adds 10 more in the first round, 20 in a quarterfinal, 30 in a semifinal and 40 in the final.

How a season runs

  • Start. A team starts at last season's final rating, after all bowl and playoff games. Think of it as six placeholder games at that rating.
  • Each game. Each real game replaces one placeholder. After six games the placeholders are gone and the rating is the plain average of this season's game scores. A team new to FBS starts at 25.
  • Re-scoring. Bonuses update as results come in, so an old win is worth more if that opponent keeps winning. A rating can move in a week the team does not play.
  • Postseason. Every bowl and playoff game counts like any other game, plus the extra points in rule 7.
  • Tiebreak. At season's end only: if two neighbors are within 1 point and the lower one won their game, they swap.

Known limits

  • 2020 is unreliable. Most conferences played only themselves, so nothing ties one conference's level to another's. The formula is not adjusted for that season.
  • A blowout of a weak team can lower a top team's rating. A rating is an average. A home blowout of an FCS team scores about 52, and a top team's average is in the mid-70s. The game takes nothing away; it earns less than the team's usual game.
  • Year-end ratings run high. Postseason points pass through opponents' bonuses, so ratings drift up by about 8 points for a typical top-40 team during the postseason.
  • The playoff field is chosen by the committee, so playoff points go to teams the committee picked.
  • The poll column has gaps. It is the AP poll until the committee's first ranking each year, then the CFP ranking. Weekly poll ranks come from that week's games, so a ranked team on a bye shows as NR.

Open formula

TrueCFB version 1.1, frozen 21 November 2026. The rules and numbers are fixed like a rulebook. They are not tuned to playoff results and not re-fit each year. Any change is a new version, with every season re-run.

The formula, the code that runs it and the exact definitions are public. The numbers on this site are rebuilt from public game results every two hours.
