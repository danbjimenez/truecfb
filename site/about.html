About TrueCFB

An open source college football ranking. The formula, the code and the data pipeline are public.

Why

Most college football rankings start with an opinion. A preseason poll ranks teams before anyone has played. A committee meets in a room and weighs what it saw.

TrueCFB starts from the scoreboard. It knows who played, where they played, who won and by how much. It does not know a team's name, logo, conference or reputation.

Every team is rated by the same seven rules. No game is dropped and no opponent gets a special case. Anyone can run the rules and get the same ranking.

It measures the season that was played. It does not predict the next game, and a gap to a poll is a measurement, not a verdict. How it works

Open source

TrueCFB is an open source project. The formula is one file of code, and the numbers on this site are rebuilt from public game results by a job anyone can read: after each week's games, after the AP poll, and after the CFP ranking.

TrueCFB 2026.2, 27 November 2026. Versions are numbered year.release: 2026.1 was released as v1.0 on 21 November 2026, and 2026.2 made margin 1 point for every 4 points, counts overtime games by their final score and set venue at 3. The rules and numbers are fixed like a rulebook. They are not tuned to playoff results and not re-fit each year. Any change is a new version, with every season re-run.

Send a change

Pull requests are welcome: bug fixes, data fixes, a clearer page, a faster one. Here is how to send one.

  1. Fork the repo. On the GitHub page, press Fork. That makes your own copy.
  2. Make your change in your copy. To check it, run the four commands under “Run it yourself” in the README.
  3. Open a pull request from your copy. The form asks three things: what you changed, whether any rating or rank moves, and how you checked it.
  4. It gets reviewed. A fix or a site improvement can be merged as it is.

A change to a rule or a number is different. The formula is fixed between versions, so that starts as an issue, with before and after rankings, and ships only as a new version.

Data and credits

Game results come from CollegeFootballData.com and ESPN, through the public sportsdataverse files. Final CFP rankings come from Wikipedia's season ranking pages.
